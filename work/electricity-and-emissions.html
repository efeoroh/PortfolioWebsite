Canada's Electricity Mix and Emissions

How Canada's power mix changed, what it did to emissions, and how Alberta's market sets the price of power.

Context

Why it mattered

Canada's power sector has cut its emissions sharply while generating about the same amount of electricity. The story is in the mix: which fuels replaced coal, which provinces still emit the most, and how Alberta's market decides which plants run.

The report uses Canada's National Greenhouse Gas Inventory (2025), Environment and Climate Change Canada's reference case projections, and Alberta Electric System Operator offer data, all processed and charted in R.

The question
How has Canada's power mix changed, what has that done to emissions, and how does Alberta's market set the price of power?
  • What replaced coal in Alberta?
  • Which provinces drive electricity emissions, now and to 2040?
  • Why do prices spike when demand is high?
Approach

How I solved it

01 · Sources

Inventory, projections, market offers.

Generation and emissions history, the federal reference case, and hourly offers from Alberta's power pool.

02 · Mix

Alberta swapped coal for gas and wind.

Coal produced over 80% of Alberta's power in the early 1990s and roughly 15 to 20% by 2023, while total output stayed just under 60 TWh.

03 · Emissions

Half the emissions, and falling.

National electricity emissions fell from just under 120 Mt in the mid-2000s to under 60 Mt by 2020, and the reference case projects about 10 to 15 Mt by the late 2030s.

04 · Provinces

A few provinces emit most of it.

Alberta accounts for about 40 to 45% of electricity emissions, Saskatchewan 20 to 25% and Ontario 15 to 20%. Hydro-heavy provinces contribute little.

05 · Merit order

Cheapest first; the last unit sets the price.

Offers are stacked from lowest to highest price. On 11 to 14 January 2024, demand sat on the steep end of the stack and the pool price climbed toward the $1,000 per MWh cap.
