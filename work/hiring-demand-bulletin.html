Context

Why it mattered

Every month, employers in Alberta post tens of thousands of jobs online. The Hiring Demand Bulletin turns those postings, alongside Statistics Canada's Labour Force Survey, into a short public report on where hiring is picking up or slowing down.

Each issue covers Alberta overall, the Calgary and Edmonton economic regions, and five occupation spotlights: trades and transport, tech, health, mining and oil and gas, and education.

The question
Where is hiring demand rising or falling this month, and in which regions, occupations and skills?
  • How many new postings were there, and how does that compare with last month and last year?
  • Which regions, occupations and employers lead?
  • What education and skills are employers asking for?
Approach

How I solved it

01 · Collect

Postings plus the survey.

Online job postings from Vicinity Jobs are paired with the month's Labour Force Survey release.

02 · Classify

Give every posting a home.

Postings are coded by occupation (NOC and TEER), economic region, skill group and instructional program (CIP).

03 · Compare

What moved since last month.

Month-over-month change in new postings by economic region, March 2025, with region names shortened. Every region rose.

04 · Locate

Where the postings are.

Over two thirds of postings concentrate in five cities.

05 · Write

Lead with the headline.

The highlights page states the total, the monthly and yearly change, and the biggest movers before any chart.

06 · Publish

Twelve pages, same shape every month.

A fixed structure means readers know where to look, and each issue is comparable with the last.
