Housing Shifts in Post-COVID Edmonton

A first-place team paper on whether COVID-19 permanently changed where Edmontonians live. I led the descriptive analysis.

Context

Why it mattered

During the pandemic, households in many large cities moved away from downtown, a pattern researchers call the donut effect. Most of that evidence comes from big, transit-rich cities.

Edmonton is different: mid-sized, car-oriented, fast-growing, and an early adopter of zoning reform. In a competition for Masters-level and above teams using City of Edmonton data, our three-person paper took first place. I led Section 5, the descriptive analysis that framed the main models.

The question
Did COVID-19 permanently change where and how people in Edmonton live?
  • Did demand shift away from the urban core?
  • Did preferences move from high-density to low-density housing?
  • Are post-2020 changes bigger than pre-pandemic trends predict?
Approach

How I solved it

01 · Data

Five million property records.

Parcel-level assessments from the City of Edmonton Open Data Portal, a confidential residential dataset with detailed land use, building permits, and context from COVID-19 counts, interest rates and CMHC.

02 · Describe

My section: describe before modelling.

Distributions, boxplots, counts by assessment class and neighbourhood maps showed a V-shaped recovery: values dipped through the pandemic and returned near their 2016 peak by 2023 to 2024.

03 · Distance

Measure the shift from the core.

Every property is placed by its distance from downtown, so changes in demand can be compared ring by ring.

04 · Density

High density against low density.

Permits and assessments by building type show housing preference moving toward low-density, peripheral homes.

05 · Counterfactual

What if COVID had not happened?

The team's Bayesian VAR forecast the post-2020 path from pre-pandemic data. Actual values moved significantly away from that forecast, pointing to a lasting structural shift.
